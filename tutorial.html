1. What is an atomic orbital?

An atomic orbital is a mathematical description of where an electron is likely to be found around a nucleus. The familiar orbital pictures are surfaces that enclose regions of relatively high electron probability.

Important: an orbital is not a track or path that an electron travels around the nucleus.

Orbital shape is associated with the angular momentum quantum number, ℓ. In this tutorial:

s → ℓ = 0    p → ℓ = 1    d → ℓ = 2

2. s orbitals: spherical symmetry

Every s orbital is spherical. The probability distribution is the same in every direction from the nucleus.

The principal quantum number n changes the size and radial structure. A 2s orbital is generally larger than a 1s orbital and contains one radial node; a 3s orbital contains two radial nodes.

Number of radial nodes = n − ℓ − 1. For an s orbital, ℓ = 0.

In the figure, the dashed circle in the 2s orbital marks a radial node: a spherical region at a certain distance from the nucleus where the probability of finding the electron is zero. The 3s orbital has two such radial nodes.

The z axis points upward. The dashed circles indicate radial nodes: one in 2s and two in 3s.

3. p orbitals: two lobes and three orientations

A p orbital has two lobes on opposite sides of the nucleus. The nucleus lies in a nodal plane where the probability density is zero.

There are three p orbitals in a p sublevel: px, py, and pz. They have the same general shape but point along different coordinate axes.

In the axis system used below, z points upward, x points horizontally, and y is drawn diagonally to suggest depth.

The same 3D axis projection is used for all three diagrams: z points up, y points up-right, and x points down-right.

4. d orbitals: five spatial patterns

A d sublevel contains five orbitals. Four have a four-lobed, cloverlike appearance. The fifth, dz², has two lobes along z plus a ring-shaped region around the center.

In the drawings below, z points upward, y points up-right, and x points down-right. The four-lobed orbitals can be distinguished by which pair of axes their lobes lie between, or which axes they point along.
